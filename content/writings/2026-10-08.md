# Are people misaligned instrumentally or terminally?

*Feels important to note, if anyone somehow happens upon this post, that the writings here so far are really unpolished musings that I don't yet want to post on Substack. They will probably lean ramble-y.*

Ever since I soft-launched pivoting to AI safety, it seems like the people around me have been feeling more and more insecure about their own life paths, especially with regards to AI. I think I felt a similar sort of insecurity for years before pivoting. 

Today someone I know tried to convince me that EAs should be focusing more effort on a much broader range of impact areas. This seems like a mild-ish claim, but after a lot of probing it really seemed like he wasn't arguing for EA exactly, but instead for instrumental rationality *given* a bunch of terminal goals. One can think of EA as having two parts: (1) a mapping from a utility function based on welfare or impartiality or something to cause prioritization, and (2) a mapping from cause prioritization to actions. This guy only wanted (2), and he wanted us to think it was fine if he just did (2) but started at his chosen cause area which was just the random thing he was studying. We kept trying to come up with real reasons why the thing he was studying was impactful to the greater world, but he seemed very not into this and insisted that he didn't want to have to make an impact case for the area; he just thought more people should be doing high impact *within* whatever areas they're currently in. 

Anyway, it just seemed kind of like cope for his current life path, speaking as someone who had life path cope for a long time. Why this path and not any other? This led to him really overestimating career transition costs. His fault, and he knew it, was not applying the same rationality towards figuring out which causes were most important in the first place. 

I also had a conversation with friends at lunch about why smart people who are somewhat ASI-pilled don't work on AI safety and do quant or whatever instead. Earning to give was mentioned but rejected because anecdotally our generation of people don't often say they're going to do this, which is good because it seemed to rarely happen in practice. Instead, my friend and I came to the conclusion that our generation is shameless and just says that they aren't utilitarian and don't have good morals and don't care about the world and just want the job with the most money, or maybe if they're being deeper, the best hopes for their kids, or maybe if they're being mopy, the best hope of escaping the permanent underclass. 

This seems not very aligned, right?

What does misalignment mean?

In this case it means that your justifications for everything just so happen to let you avoid Fear and Dealing With The Problem. You have a rabbit hole of a utility function. 

I think almost everyone is actually probably aligned terminally, but just misaligned instrumentally because of all our demons. Which... was what Yudkowsky made the whole thing of Rationality to deal with. To make people not like this. I guess he knew about Fear and these other demons that make people not do the right thing even though they know what's right. This is the problem we're facing now. 

I guess we'll see what my friends do going forward.
