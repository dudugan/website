# One big announcement

For the last few months, I've been delaying a very specific blog post. It's been more than a year since my last one - I think my longest break from blogging ever since I started in 2021. 

I've been delaying the post because I don't know how to write it well, or at least haven't taken the time to do so. I haven't posted other things in the meantime because I really want this one specific post to be *the big re-entrance* - I want this post to trigger Substack and Instagram's "this person hasn't posted yet in a while" feature. I want people to see it. To act on it. 

This is the post about my transition to AI safety. 

I guess another reason I haven't posted yet is because it feels like coming out. Often people hear that you're doing something related to AI and they immediately have a worse opinion of you, even if what you're doing is trying to make AI safer. This reaction is part of what I want the post to combat, at least among those who read my blog. 

In the past month though, it seems as if AI safety has become much more mainstream, such that this is no longer a top worry for me. The goal of the post is now probably more something like "someone you know is transitioning into AI safety, and you can too."

I keep trying to find the right title, subtitle, and first line for the post (first lines particularly matter on Substack because they show up in the preview card Substack makes for you). Here's the framing I like best so far:

```md
# updates
## ties, freedom, and life after college
I'm probably not going to grad school anymore. At least right away. This post is about why. 
```

I don't want the post to have the words "AI safety" directly on it, because I feel like that will turn people away. Instead, I want to draw people in with the type of school-concerned soul-searching posts they're used to from me (of which this will be one!). Then, a third of the way through the essay, when they're committed, I'll do the big reveal, and ask people to keep reading. 

One failure mode that I don't want to fall into is bashing linguistics. I think linguistics is really cool, and if the world gets saved, I want to go and study it. But at the same time, thinking about linguistics and grad school and impact and the future was part of what finally made me decide to transition into AI safety, so I feel like I do have to talk about it a little. 

More generally, it's really hard to toe the line between telling people AI safety is Good and Important, and making people feel bad for not transitioning into it. It might seem like what you want actually *is* to make people feel bad for not transitioning into it, but I don't think this is true. Instead, I think you want to make people feel *excited* about the possibility that they could somehow help out. There is a meaningful difference. 

I want to write: "if my post convinces even one person to transition into AI safety, I'll be happy." But this isn't totally true. In reality, my happiness will scale with the number of people the post convinces, and more convincing (e.g. someone transitions right now) is better than less convincing (e.g. people are nudged ever so slightly towards accepting future opportunities). But I think I need to stop thinking this way, and start thinking as if any amount of impact is good, because in reality, the post will probably nudge a bunch of people a tiny bit, rather than push any number of them over the edge. And it is still worth posting. 

Hopefully I know how to write it soon.
