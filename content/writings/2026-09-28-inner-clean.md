# Cleaning up and then sitting with it

All I want is a clean room and an hour each day to sit in it with. Yet as has always been the case in recent memory, there's just not enough time for all this. 

You could chalk it up to ambition, to the never-wanting-to-stop-ness that has been ingrained in many of us, and you could even say, better live with these drives than without them. 

But I don't think it's all ambition, at least not for me. I know I do my best work when I have slack - perhaps if I was properly ambitious I would throw away all my commitments and give myself some. 

Instead, it's just that there's too much. There are simply *too many things* in my room to clean up. 

I haven't posted on this blog for almost a year and a half now - my longest hiatus from blogging ever. There have been a lot of reasons, but I think many of them can be summarized as things being put in my room, and me having to clean them up. 

First it was the [eczema](https://dudugan.substack.com/p/the-trenches), sophomore fall. I moved back home, and sophomore summer my room was finally clean again, but then it happened again during Junior fall. Even worse this time. I moved off campus, but Junior winter and spring came with housing trouble. Junior June tripled the housing trouble, and added a career crisis on top. 

I only started to feel clean in July, living in a country halfway across the world, spending most of my time feeding animals. 

I only *really* felt clean at the *end* of July, back home, when all the housing stuff had been resolved, before all the applications I had to write came for me the way it seems like the Russian agents will come for Lamb in season 2 of *Slow Horses*, the way the OpenAI agents came for HuggingFace a few months ago. 

At the beginning of the semester someone asked me to summarize my summer in three words. I said, "emerging from storm." My mental image is of wind that pulls me back in like a whirlpool or a gravity well, of branches and leaves and muck flying around unpredictably around me. The air is thick and it's hard to see. I'm walking out, but my limbs move slowly as if I'm escaping a force field. It's like a movie. Pushing each leg forward takes so much effort. 

I'm out of the storm now, but there are new things that blow through my window. Subscription services. Assignments. Things that take more time than they should. [A hot take I appreciated recently](http://www.emilydardaman.com/) was "most burnout is a function of working on things that we don't think actually matter."

I feel this. I keep trying to get ahead of my schoolwork, to do a ton of it in a single week so that my later weeks will become free, but there's just too much. Maybe I could get ahead if I threw everything else to the wayside, but there are just too many things I can't throw. 

I really need to go through my email again.
